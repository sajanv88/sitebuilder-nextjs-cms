A redirect sends visitors from an old address to a new one, so links people saved, shared or found in a search engine keep working. **Redirects** in the sidebar lists them all. Editors and admins can use it.

<Screenshot src={redirects} alt="The Redirects screen listing four redirects: /news/* to /blog/*, /our-team to /about-us/our-team, /shop to /visit-the-shop, and /summer-menu to /our-bread, for now." />

## The ones Sitebuilder adds for you

Most redirects you'll never write yourself. Sitebuilder adds one whenever an address that visitors may know changes:

- you change the slug of a published page or post, or move a page under another;
- you change the address of the blog in Settings → Reading;
- you change the slug of a category or tag.

They're listed here with the rest, and you can change or remove them like any other.

## Add a redirect

Add your own when you move to Sitebuilder from another site, so its old addresses lead somewhere, or when a page is gone and another one covers it.

1. Press **Add redirect**.
2. In **From**, type the old address on your site, starting with `/`, such as `/shop`.
3. In **To**, type where it should go: an address on your site such as `/visit-the-shop`, or another site's address starting with `https://`.
4. Choose **How** it redirects (see below), and press **Add redirect**.

<Screenshot src={addRedirect} alt="Add a redirect: From /old-blog/*, To /blog/*, How Moved for good (301)." />

### A whole section at once

End **From** with `/*` to cover everything under an address. `/old-blog/*` catches `/old-blog`, `/old-blog/2024/our-first-year` and anything else under it.

End **To** with `/*` too to keep the rest of the address: with `/old-blog/*` to `/blog/*`, `/old-blog/2024/our-first-year` goes to `/blog/2024/our-first-year`. Without it, every address under `/old-blog` goes to the one address in **To**.

### For good or for now

- **Moved for good (301)** is right almost every time. Search engines move what they know about the old address to the new one.
- **Moved for now (302)** is for a short while, such as a seasonal page you'll bring back. Search engines keep the old address.
- The **308** and **307** versions do the same, and also pass on a form someone was sending. You'll rarely need them.

## What comes first

A page or post always wins over a redirect. A redirect only answers at an address where there's nothing else, so you can't redirect away from a page that's live. Sitebuilder tells you if you try: move the page, or choose another address.

It also stops you making a loop, such as `/a` to `/b` and `/b` back to `/a`, and keeps out addresses that belong to Sitebuilder itself, such as `/admin`.

## Change or remove one

The **⋯** menu at the end of each row has **Edit** and **Remove**. Once a redirect is removed, its old address shows "page not found" again.

Search the list, or click a column heading to sort by it.
