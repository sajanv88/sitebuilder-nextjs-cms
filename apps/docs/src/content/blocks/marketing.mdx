The blocks for a page that sells something: a big opening, what you offer, what it costs, and why people should believe you. They're under **Marketing** in the **Add** list. The pictures on this page come from one example page, for the bakery's bread club.

## Hero

The top of a landing page: a short line, a headline, a sentence, up to two buttons and a picture under them.

<Screenshot src={hero} alt="A hero with a small Bread club label, the headline A fresh loaf on your table every week, a sentence, Join the club and See our bread buttons, and a picture of a loaf under them." />

- **Line over the headline**: optional, a few words in a small label.
- **Headline**: the one line that says what this is.
- **Sentence under it**: optional, who it's for and why it helps.
- **Main button** and **Second button**: turn on **Show this button**, then fill in its words and where it goes. The main one is filled with your brand color; the second is quieter.
- **Picture**: optional, shown under the text.
- **Alignment**, **Width on the page**, **Height**, **Content's place** and the **moving backdrop** settings work as they do in a [Section](/blocks/layout#section).

A hero at the very top of a page takes the page's place for a title: its headline becomes the page's main heading, and the page title isn't shown above it. Anywhere else on the page, it's a section heading like any other.

## Feature grid

Cards with an icon, a title and a sentence each, for the reasons to choose you. Up to 12.

<Screenshot src={featureGrid} alt="A feature grid headed Why join, with three cards: No queue with a clock, Members' bread with a heart, and Pause any week with a calendar." />

- **Heading**: optional, above the cards.
- **Features**: the cards. Each has an **Icon** (search the list by name), a **Title** and a **Sentence**.
- **Layout**: **Grid** shows every card at once; **Carousel** shows a few at a time. A carousel hides most cards, so keep what everyone must see in a grid.
- **Card background**: the cards' own look, **Muted**, your **Brand** color, or any **Colour** you pick. The text and icon change to stay readable on it.

## Pricing

Plans side by side, each with its price, what's included and a button. Up to four.

<Screenshot src={pricing} alt="Three plans: One loaf at £4 a week, Family at £10 a week marked Recommended with a blue Choose button, and Café at £45 a week with Talk to us." />

For each plan:

- **Name** and **Price**, written as you want it shown: £10, Free, $99.
- **Period**: optional, such as "a week" or "a month".
- **What's included**: a line per feature, up to 12, each with a tick.
- **Button**: its words and where it goes.
- **Recommend this plan**: marks it **Recommended**, with a colored edge and a filled button. Recommend one plan at most.

## Questions and answers

Questions that open to show their answers, up to 20. Opening one closes the one that was open. The answers are in the page even while they're closed, so search engines can read them.

<Screenshot src={faq} alt="Three questions: the first, Can I change my bread?, is open with its answer; What if I can't collect? and How do I pay? are closed." />

Each one has a **Question** and an **Answer**. Write the questions the way your customers ask them.

## Reviews

What customers said, with their names and stars, up to 12.

<Screenshot src={reviews} alt="Three reviews in cards, each with stars, what the customer said, and their name: Priya, Tom, and Jo and Alex." />

- **What they said**: their words, up to 500 characters.
- **Name**, and **Role or company** if it helps.
- **Stars**: 1 to 5.
- **Layout**: **Grid** or **Carousel**, as in Feature grid.

Use real reviews, with permission. Made-up ones are easy to spot, and in many countries against the law.

## Figures

Up to four big numbers, such as how many customers you've served.

<Screenshot src={figures} alt="Three figures under the heading The club so far: 340 Members, 18,000+ Loaves collected, and 4.9/5 Average rating." />

For each figure:

- **Number**: the figure itself. It's written the way your site's language writes numbers, so 18000 shows as 18,000 in English.
- **Before the number** and **After the number**: optional, short: a currency sign before, or +, % or /5 after.
- **What it counts**: the label under it.
- **A line about it**: optional, a sentence more.

**Roll the numbers into place** counts them up once, as they come into view. Visitors who've asked their device for less motion see the final numbers straight away. **Heading** and **Alignment** are optional.
