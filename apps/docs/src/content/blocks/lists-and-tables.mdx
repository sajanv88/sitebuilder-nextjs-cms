Blocks for information with a shape to it: your newest posts, rows and columns, and events in order. They're under **Lists and tables** in the **Add** list.

## Latest posts

Your newest posts, kept up to date by themselves. Publish a post and it appears here; you never edit this block to add one.

<Screenshot src={latestPosts} alt="Three posts in cards, newest first: Meet our new oven, Christmas orders are open, and Rye on Tuesdays too, each with its date and a sentence." />

- **How many**: 1 to 24 posts.
- **Only from these categories and tags**: leave it empty for every post, or pick some to show only posts that have one of them, such as the News category.
- **Layout**: **Cards** side by side, or a **List**.
- **Show each post's excerpt**: the short summary under each title. A post without a summary of its own shows none.

Only published posts are listed, newest first. Drafts and scheduled posts join the list when they go live.

## Table

Rows and columns of text, such as a price list or opening hours.

<Screenshot src={table} alt="A table of bread prices with three columns, Bread, Small and Large, and rows for sourdough, rye and white, with the caption Bread prices under it." />

- **Caption**: what the table shows. It's written under the table, and screen readers say it as the table's name.
- **Columns**: a heading for each column, up to 8.
- **Rows**: up to 100, with a box for each column. **Add a row** puts one at the end, and **Remove row** takes one out.

The first cell of each row names that row, as Sourdough, Rye and White do in the picture, so make the first column the one people look along.

On a narrow screen, a wide table scrolls sideways rather than squashing its text.

## Timeline

Dated events in order, such as your story or a project's milestones. Up to 20.

<Screenshot src={timeline} alt="A timeline headed Our story, with four events down a line: 2018 We find the shop, May 2019 We open, 2023 The bread club starts, 2026 A second oven." />

- **Heading**: optional, above the events.
- **When**: written as you'd say it, such as 2019 or March 2024. It isn't a date picker, so "Spring 2020" works too.
- **What happened**: a short title.
- **More about it**: optional, a sentence or two.

The events are shown in the order you put them, not sorted by date, so use **Earlier** and **Later** to arrange them.
