The first account on a new site is an admin, and it's the only one you make without signing in. Everyone else you add later, from Users.

## Fill in the setup form

1. Open your site's address followed by `/setup`. On your own computer that's [http://localhost:3000/setup](http://localhost:3000/setup).
2. Type your name and your email address. The name is what other people see next to your pages and posts.
3. Choose a password of at least 12 characters.
4. Press **Create the admin account**.

<Screenshot src={setupForm} alt="The Set up your site form, with Your name, Email and Password filled in, and the Create the admin account button." />

<Note title="Setup only works once">
  As soon as a site has an account, `/setup` shows a "not found" page. Nobody who finds the address later can make themselves an admin.
</Note>

## Sign in

Sitebuilder takes you to the sign-in page, with a line saying the site is set up. Type the email and password you just chose and press **Sign in**.

<Screenshot src={signIn} alt="The Sign in form with Email and Password fields and a Sign in button." />

From now on you sign in at your site's address followed by `/admin`. If you aren't signed in, any admin page sends you here first.

There's no "Forgot your password?" link yet. It appears once an admin has set up email in **Settings → Email**, because Sitebuilder needs a way to send you the reset link.

## Your dashboard

After signing in you land on the dashboard. On a new site everything reads zero.

<Screenshot src={dashboard} alt="A new site's dashboard: four counts at zero, an empty Recent changes list, and a Get started checklist at 0 of 6 done." />

The **Get started** card on the right lists six steps to a site that's ready for visitors. Nothing on it is ticked by hand: each step ticks itself when the site really has it, and the card goes away when all six are done.

The next three pages of this guide cover four of the steps: naming the site, publishing a page, choosing the home page and building the menu. Templates and the look of the site have their own guides.
